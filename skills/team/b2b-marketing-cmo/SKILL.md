# B2B Marketing CMO

Full-spectrum marketing strategy and execution for Team's B2B products. From strategic frameworks through execution checklists.

## Workflow Overview

```
PHASE 1: STRATEGY         → Framework application (4Ps, STP, AIDA, Porter's)
PHASE 2: INTELLIGENCE     → Market research, competitive analysis, pricing research
PHASE 3: POSITIONING      → Value proposition, differentiation, messaging architecture
PHASE 4: CAMPAIGN DESIGN  → Integrated campaigns, channel mix, content calendar
PHASE 5: ENABLEMENT       → Sales tools, battle cards, objection handling
PHASE 6: EXECUTION        → Playbooks with team checklists, timelines, quality gates
PHASE 7: OPTIMIZATION     → Performance tracking, ROI analysis, adjustment
```

## Phase 1: Strategic Framework Application

### 1.1 Framework Selection Matrix

| Objective | Primary Framework | Supporting Frameworks |
|-----------|-------------------|----------------------|
| New product launch | 4Ps → STP → AIDA | Blue Ocean, Ansoff |
| Market expansion | Ansoff Matrix → Porter's 5 | PESTLE, STP |
| Competitive response | Porter's 5 Forces → Positioning | Perceptual mapping |
| Pricing strategy | Value-based pricing → Competitive | 4Ps (Price pillar) |
| Campaign design | AIDA → Content mapping | STP targeting |
| International entry | PESTLE → Ansoff | Risk-reward analysis |

### 1.2 The 4Ps Framework

See [`references/strategic-frameworks.md`](references/strategic-frameworks.md) for complete templates.

**For each product, document:**

**PRODUCT**
- Core offering and feature set
- Tiered versions (if applicable)
- Integration points with ecosystem
- Delivery model (SaaS, project, hybrid)

**PRICE**
- Pricing model (subscription, usage, project, hybrid)
- Price points and tier structure
- Discounting policy
- Payment terms (Bangladesh market: flexible typically required)

**PLACE**
- Sales channels (direct, partner, hybrid)
- Geographic focus (Bangladesh primary, international opportunistic)
- Delivery mechanism
- Support structure

**PROMOTION**
- Key messages and positioning
- Channel mix
- Campaign types
- Content strategy

### 1.3 STP Framework

**SEGMENTATION** — Define market segments by:
- Industry vertical (FMCG, Healthcare, Real Estate, Research, Defense)
- Company size (SME 100-500 employees is sweet spot)
- Geographic (Dhaka-centric, regional, international)
- Decision-making maturity (sophisticated vs. emerging)
- Technology adoption stage

**TARGETING** — For each segment, assess:
- Segment size and growth
- Competitive intensity
- Fit with capabilities
- Relationship access strength
- Revenue potential
- Strategic alignment

**POSITIONING** — Define:
- Target segment(s)
- Frame of reference
- Point of differentiation
- Reason to believe
- Emotional benefit

### 1.4 AIDA Framework for Campaign Design

Map content and touchpoints to each stage:

| Stage | Objective | Content Types | Channels | Metrics |
|-------|-----------|---------------|----------|---------|
| **Awareness** | Know we exist | Thought leadership, industry commentary | LinkedIn, events, PR | Reach, impressions |
| **Interest** | Understand value | Case studies, capability demos, webinars | LinkedIn, email, website | Engagement, time on site |
| **Desire** | Want our solution | ROI calculators, pilot offers, testimonials | Direct sales, email nurture | Qualified leads, meetings |
| **Action** | Sign contract | Proposals, negotiations, onboarding | Direct sales | Conversion, deal velocity |

### 1.5 Additional Strategic Frameworks

**Porter's Five Forces** — Assess industry attractiveness:
- Competitive rivalry
- Supplier power
- Buyer power
- Threat of substitutes
- Threat of new entrants

**PESTLE Analysis** — External environment scanning:
- Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Legal, Environmental

**Ansoff Matrix** — Growth strategy selection:
- Market penetration, Market development, Product development, Diversification

**Blue Ocean Strategy** — Differentiation:
- Eliminate, Reduce, Raise, Create grid

See [`references/strategic-frameworks.md`](references/strategic-frameworks.md) for templates and examples.

## Phase 2: Market Intelligence

### 2.1 Research Protocol

**Minimum requirements for any analysis:**
- 8+ distinct web searches
- 3+ source types (reports, news, company info)
- Date currency (prefer data from last 12 months)
- Bangladesh-specific data where available

### 2.2 Competitive Analysis Workflow

1. **Identify competitors** (direct, indirect, alternative solutions)
2. **Gather intelligence**:
   - Company overview and history
   - Product offerings and pricing (if available)
   - Target market and positioning
   - Key clients and case studies
   - Recent news and developments
   - Strengths and weaknesses
3. **Map competitive landscape** using positioning map
4. **Identify differentiation opportunities**

See [`references/market-intelligence-protocols.md`](references/market-intelligence-protocols.md) for templates.

### 2.3 TAM/SAM/SOM Calculation

**TAM (Total Addressable Market)**
- Total market size for the solution category
- Use industry reports, government data, extrapolation

**SAM (Serviceable Addressable Market)**
- TAM filtered by:
  - Geographic focus (Bangladesh, target international markets)
  - Industry verticals serves
  - Company size we can effectively serve

**SOM (Serviceable Obtainable Market)**
- SAM filtered by:
  - Realistic market capture rate (typically 5-20% in Year 3)
  - Competitive positioning
  - Sales capacity constraints

### 2.4 Buyer Persona Development

For each product, document:
- **Job title and role**
- **Goals and success metrics**
- **Pain points and challenges**
- **Decision-making process**
- **Information sources**
- **Objections and concerns**
- **Influence on buying decision**

See [`references/product-profiles.md`](references/product-profiles.md) for existing persona work.

## Phase 3: Pricing Strategy

### 3.1 Pricing Research Protocol

**When pricing needs to be determined (Forge, TradeEye):**

1. **Competitive benchmarking**
   - Search: "[product type] pricing Bangladesh"
   - Search: "[product type] software pricing"
   - Document competitor pricing models and levels

2. **Value-based analysis**
   - Quantify customer benefit (cost savings, revenue increase)
   - Calculate economic value to customer
   - Determine price-to-value ratio

3. **Cost-plus analysis**
   - Calculate delivery costs
   - Add margin requirements
   - Validate against value-based ceiling

4. **Willingness-to-pay research**
   - Analyze RFP budgets from pipeline
   - Reference similar deals
   - Use relationship intelligence

### 3.2 Pricing Model Selection

| Model | Best For | Products |
|-------|----------|---------------------|
| **Subscription (SaaS)** | Recurring value, predictable delivery | Proggya, Data Dialogue |
| **Campaign-based** | Discrete projects, variable scope | ICE |
| **Enterprise licensing** | Large-scale deployments | TradeEye |
| **Hybrid** | Complex solutions, multiple components | Forge (training + platform) |

### 3.3 Pricing Strategy Frameworks

**Value-Based Pricing Steps:**
1. Identify customer segment
2. Determine next best alternative
3. Quantify differentiation value
4. Set price at % of value created (typically 10-30%)
5. Validate with market data

**Tiered Pricing Design:**
- Good-Better-Best structure
- Clear upgrade path
- Anchor with premium tier
- Standard tier as target

See [`references/pricing-strategy-guide.md`](references/pricing-strategy-guide.md) for complete methodology.

## Phase 6: Sales Enablement

### 6.1 Battle Card Components

For each product, create:
- **Product overview** (1-paragraph description)
- **Target customer profile**
- **Key pain points addressed**
- **Competitive positioning** (vs. 3-5 alternatives)
- **Objection handling** (5-8 common objections)
- **Discovery questions** (10-15 qualifying questions)
- **Proof points** (case studies, statistics)
- **Pricing guidance**
- **Next steps** (typical sales process)

### 6.2 Objection Handling Framework

| Objection Type | Strategy | Example Response |
|----------------|----------|------------------|
| **Price** | Reframe to value/ROI | "The question isn't cost, it's return..." |
| **Timing** | Create urgency, reduce risk | "Pilot program available..." |
| **Competition** | Differentiate, don't disparage | "Unlike [competitor], we..." |
| **Authority** | Enable the champion | "Here's a business case template..." |
| **Need** | Problem amplification | "Companies without this typically see..." |
| **Trust** | Social proof | "BATB has been using us since..." |

### 6.3 Case Study Template

### 8.2 ROI Tracking Metrics

**Leading Indicators:**

- LinkedIn follower growth

- Email open and click rates
- Event attendance

**Lagging Indicators:**
- Marketing Qualified Leads (MQLs)
- Sales Qualified Leads (SQLs)
- Opportunity creation
- Pipeline value
- Closed revenue
- Customer acquisition cost (CAC)
- Marketing-attributed revenue

### 8.3 CAC and LTV Modeling

**Customer Acquisition Cost:**
```
CAC = (Marketing Spend + Sales Spend) / New Customers
```

**Lifetime Value:**
```
LTV = Average Monthly Revenue × Gross Margin × Average Customer Lifespan (months)
```

**Target Ratio:** LTV:CAC > 3:1

## Phase 9: International Market Assessment

### 9.1 Market Entry Framework

When assessing international expansion (Iberia, UAE, ANZ, Africa):

**Market Attractiveness Score:**
- Market size and growth (weight: 25%)
- Competitive intensity (weight: 20%)
- Regulatory complexity (weight: 15%)
- Cultural/language fit (weight: 15%)
- Partnership availability (weight: 15%)
- Cost of entry (weight: 10%)

**Readiness Score:**
- Product-market fit (weight: 30%)
- Team capability (weight: 25%)
- Financial resources (weight: 25%)
- Local network (weight: 20%)

### 9.2 Market Entry Modes

| Mode | Investment | Control | Risk | Time to Market |
|------|------------|---------|------|----------------|
| **Remote delivery** | Low | High | Low | Fast |
| **Local partner** | Medium | Medium | Medium | Medium |
| **Distributor/reseller** | Low | Low | Medium | Fast |
| **Local office** | High | High | High | Slow |
| **Acquisition** | Very High | High | High | Medium |

### 9.3 Go/No-Go Decision Matrix

| Score Range | Recommendation |
|-------------|----------------|
| 80-100 | Strong go — prioritize entry |
| 60-79 | Conditional go — address gaps first |
| 40-59 | Hold — monitor for changes |
| 0-39 | No go — deprioritize |

## Dynamic Research Triggers

### When Pricing Needs Research

**Triggers:**
- New product without established pricing (Forge, TradeEye)
- Entering new market segment
- Competitive pricing changes detected
- RFP with unusual requirements
- Customer pushback on current pricing

**Action:**
- Trigger web research on comparable products
- Analyze RFP budgets from pipeline
- Calculate value-based ceiling
- Recommend pricing with confidence interval
- Flag for CEO validation

### When Delivery Model Needs Assessment

**Triggers:**
- Product could be SaaS or project-based
- Customer requesting non-standard terms
- Scaling challenges with current model
- Competitive pressure on model

**Action:**
- Analyze customer preference data
- Assess operational capability for each model
- Model unit economics for each approach
- Recommend with pros/cons
- Flag for strategic decision

### When International Opportunity Arises

**Triggers:**
- Inbound inquiry from target geography
- Partner opportunity in new market
- Competitive threat in domestic market
- Available bandwidth to expand

**Action:**
- Run market attractiveness assessment
- Assess readiness score
- Recommend entry mode
- Estimate investment required
- Flag for strategic review
