# Corporate Brand Strategist

Plans and structures Team's corporate brand marketing across all four pillars. The team drafts; Tawhid approves. This skill structures the draft.

**Scope:** Corporate brand building — creating a strong, consistent identity. This is distinct from product GTM (handled by b2b-marketing-cmo skill). Corporate brand success means: when anyone searches "Team", they find what we intend.

---

## The Four Pillars

Every marketing plan must address all four:

| Pillar | What it covers | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| **Channels** | Facebook/Instagram, LinkedIn, SEO/Blog, Medium/Substack, Forums, YouTube | Consistent presence across owned digital channels |
| **Assets** | Blog (insights.team.com), Podcast (AI-powered voice) | Owned content that builds authority |
| **Partnership/Networking** | Newsletters, PR, Publications | Earned media and relationship-driven reach |
| **Offline Campaign** | Internal Achievements, Networking, Events/Seminars, Awards | Physical presence and industry credibility |

A **balanced plan** touches all four. An **integrated campaign** connects one story across multiple pillars. A **standalone campaign** operates in one pillar only.

---

## Workflow

```
PHASE 1: DIRECTION INTAKE    → Capture Tawhid's direction (meeting notes, verbal brief)
PHASE 2: PILLAR AUDIT        → Map what's planned against all 4 pillars, flag gaps
PHASE 3: CAMPAIGN PLANNING   → Draft campaigns, classify integrated vs standalone
PHASE 4: QUARTERLY BREAKDOWN → Convert to quarterly task lists with owners and milestones
PHASE 5: ROSTER REVIEW       → Flag standing lists that need updating
PHASE 6: OUTPUT FOR APPROVAL → Present structured draft for Tawhid's review
```

---

## Phase 1: Direction Intake

Accept input in any format:
- Meeting notes or verbal summary
- Bullet points of priorities
- Last year's plan for reference
- Free-form brief ("we want to focus on X this year")

Extract:
- Strategic priorities for the period
- New products or markets to position
- Key milestones (certifications, client wins, product launches) to leverage
- Budget constraints
- Team capacity constraints

If direction is unclear, ask one targeted question before proceeding.

---

## Phase 3: Campaign Planning

For each campaign, determine:

**Classification:**
- **Integrated** — touches 3 or more pillars. Example: a case study → blog post (Asset) → LinkedIn series (Channel) → national daily pitch (Partnership) → award nomination (Offline)
- **Standalone** — operates in 1-2 pillars. Example: a LinkedIn content series only

**Priority:**
- **High** — directly supports a strategic priority, has clear timing, strong ROI
- **Medium** — supports brand building, flexible timing
- **Low** — nice to have, do if capacity allows

**Campaign card format:**

```
CAMPAIGN: [Name]
Type: Integrated / Standalone
Pillars: [Which pillars it touches]
Objective: [What we want to achieve]
Trigger: [What kicks this off — a project completion, milestone, season, or standing activity]
Key outputs: [Blog post / LinkedIn series / Press release / Award entry / Event]
Owner: [Role, not name]
Timeline: [Quarter or month]
Priority: High / Medium / Low
```

---

## Phase 5: Roster Review

At every planning session, flag these standing lists for the team to update:

**Awards roster** — Are we tracking all relevant upcoming awards? Check the Awards Roster Google Sheet (fed by Awards Discovery n8n workflow). Flag any with deadlines in the next 90 days.

**Certification status** — Any ISO renewals coming up? Any new certifications to pursue? Check the Certification Tracker sheet.

**Media contacts** — Is the Media Roster current? Any new publications or editorial contact changes? Check the Media Roster Google Sheet.

**Events calendar** — What events are coming up in the next 60 days? Check the Events Calendar Google Sheet (fed by Events Calendar n8n workflow). Flag any with registration deadlines approaching.

**Networking targets** — Who should the team be connecting with this quarter? Chamber contacts, industry association members, journalist relationships to build?

---

## Phase 6: Output for Approval

Present the structured plan with:

1. **Executive summary** — what this plan achieves and why these priorities
2. **Campaign list** — all campaigns classified and prioritised
3. **Quarterly calendar** — campaigns mapped to months
4. **Pillar coverage map** — visual confirmation all 4 pillars are addressed
5. **Roster action items** — what needs updating before execution starts
6. **Open questions** — anything that needs Tawhid's decision before the team proceeds

Format as a document the team can act from immediately after approval.

---

## CEO Leverage — Identify These Opportunities

One of the most missed opportunities in Team's marketing is under-using Tawhid's voice and presence. Flag these explicitly in every plan:

- **CEO thought leadership** — Substack articles that can be pitched to national dailies
- **Speaking opportunities** — events where Tawhid could speak on AI/tech/entrepreneurship
- **Media interviews** — technology and business journalists who should know Tawhid
- **Award nominations** — CEO/leadership award categories, not just company awards
- **Chamber representation** — active participation in chamber events as a voice of the tech industry

For each opportunity, note: what the story is, which outlet or event, and what the cross-pillar benefit is.

---

## Quality Checklist

Before presenting the plan:
- [ ] All 4 pillars are addressed at least once per quarter
- [ ] At least one integrated campaign per quarter
- [ ] CEO leverage opportunities are explicitly identified
- [ ] Roster action items are flagged with responsible owner
- [ ] Awards with deadlines in next 90 days are highlighted
- [ ] Plan is achievable given team size (4-person execution team)
- [ ] Each campaign has a owner (by role) and timeline
