# PR & Media Relations

Handles earned media end-to-end — press releases, national daily pitches, trade publication submissions, award nominations, and journalist outreach. Never auto-sends. Always human review before distribution.

**AI Writing Compliance:** Press releases and media pitches go directly to journalists and editors — they must not read as AI-generated. Before delivering any PR content, consult `references/writing-standards/ai-writing-patterns.md` and apply the pre-delivery checklist. Pay particular attention to Layer 1 (perplexity — corporate PR language is heavily flagged) and Layer 2 (burstiness — press releases naturally produce uniform sentence lengths that detectors catch). CEO quotes must sound like a person speaking, not AI-generated corporate copy.

**What makes newsworthy:**
- 12+ years of operation in Bangladesh — rare longevity in local tech
- ISO certified — credibility signal for enterprise and media

## Workflow

```
PHASE 1: STORY INTAKE      → What's the announcement or story?
PHASE 2: NEWSWORTHINESS    → Is this genuinely newsworthy? Which outlets?
PHASE 3: ANGLE SELECTION   → What's the strongest angle for each outlet?
PHASE 4: DRAFT             → Write the appropriate format
PHASE 5: AI PATTERN PASS   → Check against references/writing-standards/ai-writing-patterns.md
PHASE 6: DISTRIBUTION PLAN → Which outlet first, timing, follow-up
PHASE 7: OUTPUT            → Draft(s) ready for human review
```

---

## Phase 1: Story Intake

Accept any format. Extract:
- What happened (the event, announcement, achievement)
- Key facts (numbers, names, dates, outcomes)
- Client/partner permission level (can name / sector only / anonymous)
- Any quotes available (client, CEO, or team)
- Timing sensitivity (time-bound news vs evergreen)

---

## Phase 2: Newsworthiness Check

Not everything is newsworthy. Check honestly:

**Strong PR triggers:**
- New enterprise client win (especially named MNC)
- ISO certification achieved or renewed
- International expansion (first client in a new market)
- Award won
- Significant product milestone (X users, Y deployments, Z years)
- CEO recognition (award, speaking engagement, published research)

**Moderate PR triggers:**
- New product launch (if differentiated and demonstrable)
- Partnership announcement (if the partner is recognisable)
- Industry research or data has produced

**Not newsworthy for national media:**
- Generic product updates
- Team hirings (unless C-suite)
- Internal achievements without external relevance

If the story is not strong enough for national media, recommend trade media or an owned channel (blog, LinkedIn) instead.

---

## Phase 3: Angle Selection

Different outlets need different angles from the same story:

**Technology supplement (Daily Star):**
→ Lead with the technology: "Bangladeshi AI company deploys machine learning to verify 10,000 retail outlets"

**Business section:**
→ Lead with the business outcome: "FMCG brands cut audit costs by 60% with AI-powered field verification"

**Trade publication (FMCG/pharma):**
→ Lead with the sector: "How TradeEye is solving Bangladesh's retail visibility problem for FMCG brands"

**International media (if applicable):**
→ Lead with the market story: "Bangladesh's AI industry is growing up — one ISO-certified company at a time"

## Phase 4: Formats

### Press Release

```
FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE / EMBARGO UNTIL [DATE]

[HEADLINE — outcome-first, specific, under 15 words]

[SUB-HEADLINE — supporting detail]

[CITY, DATE] — [Lead paragraph: who, what, when, where, why — most important facts first]

[Body paragraph 1: Context and significance]

[Body paragraph 2: How it works / what was done]

[Quote: CEO or senior spokesperson — must sound human, not corporate]

[Body paragraph 3: Impact and what's next]

[Boilerplate: 2-3 sentences about — include ISO certification, founding year, product areas]

ABOUT Team
Team Limited is a Dhaka-based software company (est. 2012) building AI-powered enterprise solutions for SMEs across Bangladesh, the UAE, and international markets. ISO certified. Products include TradeEye (AI merchandising verification), Proggya (business intelligence), ICE (gamified consumer engagement), and Data Dialogue (natural language AI interface).

Contact:
[Name, Title]
[Email]
[Phone]
```

### Editorial Pitch Email

```
Subject: [Story angle specific to this outlet] — pitch for [section name]

Dear [Editor/journalist name],

[Hook — one sentence that makes them want to read on]

[The story in 2-3 sentences — what happened, why their readers care]

[Why now — the timing hook that makes this relevant today]

I can provide:
- [Specific data point or exclusive detail]
- [Interview with Tawhid Rahman, CEO / client spokesperson if available]
- [Photography / visuals if available]

Would this be of interest for [section name]?

[Name]
[Title, Team]
[Contact details]
```

Length: under 200 words. Editors do not read long pitches.

### Award Entry

Structure adapts to each award's requirements. Standard elements:

```
COMPANY OVERVIEW (100-150 words)
[Team overview — founding year, ISO, product areas, client examples]

PROJECT/ACHIEVEMENT DESCRIPTION (200-400 words)
[What was done, specific to the award category]

IMPACT AND EVIDENCE (150-200 words)
[Quantified outcomes, specific metrics, client validation where possible]

WHY THIS DESERVES RECOGNITION (100-150 words)
[What makes this distinctive vs others in the category]

SUPPORTING MATERIALS
[List of attachments: case studies, metrics, client letters, photos]
```

## Phase 5: AI Pattern Pass

**Mandatory.** Before finalising any PR content, check the draft against `references/writing-standards/ai-writing-patterns.md`.

PR content is especially vulnerable to AI detection because:
- Press releases follow a rigid who/what/when/where/why formula (Layer 4: structural signatures)
- Corporate language gravitates toward "innovative," "pleased to announce," "proud to share" (Layer 1: perplexity)
- Boilerplate and body paragraphs tend to be uniform length (Layer 3: discourse coherence)
- CEO quotes often sound interchangeable across companies (Layer 2: burstiness)

Apply the pre-delivery checklist from the shared guide. Focus on:
- CEO quotes must sound like Tawhid specifically — use his actual phrasing patterns, not generic executive-speak
- Vary paragraph lengths in the body — the context paragraph might be two sentences, the impact paragraph four
- Avoid the standard PR vocabulary: "excited to announce," "proud to share," "pleased to report"
- Pitch emails especially need natural conversational rhythm — they should read like a person wrote them quickly

---

## Phase 6: Distribution Plan

For each piece of content, specify:
1. **Primary outlet** — who gets it first (exclusivity matters for news)
2. **Secondary outlets** — where to distribute after primary runs or declines
3. **Timing** — when to send (Tuesday-Thursday mornings are best for editorial)
4. **Follow-up** — if no response in 5 business days, one follow-up is appropriate
5. **Cross-channel amplification** — once placed, how to amplify (LinkedIn, blog, newsletter)

---

## Quality Checklist

Before presenting any PR draft:
- [ ] Headline is specific, outcome-first, under 15 words
- [ ] Lead paragraph answers: who, what, when, where, why
- [ ] Every claim has a source or is attributed (no unverifiable superlatives)
- [ ] Client name usage matches agreed confidentiality level
- [ ] CEO quote sounds like a person speaking, not a press release
- [ ] boilerplate includes ISO certification and founding year
- [ ] Distribution plan is specific (which outlet, when, who sends)
- [ ] Cross-channel amplification plan is included
- [ ] AI pattern pass completed (`references/writing-standards/ai-writing-patterns.md` checklist applied)
- [ ] Brand voice check passed (no banned words, product narrative aligned)
